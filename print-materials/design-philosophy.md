# Porch Light

**A design philosophy for warmth that can be read from across the road.**

Porch Light begins with a single image: a lamp left on at the edge of a dark field, the one fixed point that says *someone is home, come in*. Every composition is organized around one luminous point of arrival. Everything else recedes into calm, generous fields of color so that the eye has exactly one place to land. Nothing competes with it. That restraint is the work of painstaking editing, the product of deep expertise in knowing what to leave out.

Space is the dominant material. Broad, quiet planes of deep pine and warm paper hold the composition the way a porch holds the evening. They are wide, unhurried and steady. Forms are perfect and few: circles that radiate outward in slow concentric rings, clean bands that divide the canvas into calm zones, and small repeated marks placed at exact intervals like fence posts along a county road. Each ring, each interval is meticulously spaced, as though measured by hand and checked again.

Color is limited to three voices. Pine green carries depth and trust. Ember orange is used sparingly, only where warmth must be felt: the light itself. Cream paper lets everything breathe. The palette never grows. Tints appear only as quiet echoes of these three, calibrated with master-level care so that the whole reads as one warm, unified surface rather than a collection of parts.

Typography is plainspoken and large where it matters, whispered where it doesn't. A classic serif carries the voice of a trusted neighbor, and a clean geometric sans carries numbers and instructions with total clarity. Scale does the talking. One line is monumental, a few are small and exact, and nothing is ornamental. Letterforms are set with obsessive attention to spacing and alignment, so the type feels carved rather than typed.

Rhythm comes from repetition: rows of identical rounded marks, evenly stepped rings, a disciplined grid that never breaks. The eye moves in a slow, predictable path, from light, to invitation, to the single action. The finished piece should look like the result of countless hours of refinement by someone at the very top of their craft. It should be humble in tone, immaculate in execution, and legible to anyone at a glance.
